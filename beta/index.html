How it works, word for word8 steps · the chain · 4 signal stages

A pothole jolts every vehicle that crosses it, and the moment is gone as soon as the wheel rolls on. Nobody writes down where it was, how hard it hit, or whether it is still there tomorrow. Pothole Observer keeps that moment. Scroll to ride over two real bumps.

The chain

  • Road: A pothole, a speed breaker
  • Vibration: The wheel drops, the frame shakes
  • Phone: The phone in its holder feels it
  • Detection: The app decides what it was
  • Location: GPS pins where it happened
  • Shared map: Every ride adds to one map

Your phone already feels the road. Pothole Observer teaches it what that feeling means. No camera and no extra hardware: the motion sensors every Android phone carries, a little signal processing, and two small forests of decision trees that run on the phone during the ride.

The 8 steps · from motion to meaning, one real jolt of the 19:06 bike ride

    Signal stages, as recorded

    • Raw readings · a = (x, y, z): Accelerometer, in the phone's own axes. Gravity is in there too.
    • Gravity removed · a · ĝ − |g|: Projected onto gravity, then gravity taken away: up-and-down motion only.
    • Filtered · 0.5–20 Hz: How these charts draw it: what the road feels like, without slow tilts.
    • Triggered · |sharp| > max(1.5, 3.5 σ): The sharp part crosses the trigger line (this ride: 3.5 × the road's usual shaking σ): a candidate.

      Inside the model, word for word6 stages · 4 notes

      What happens between a bump and a dot on the map? Follow one real jolt from the 19:06 bike ride through the model the app ships today. Every number below was computed by running that model on the jolt you pick.

      • 01 · Trigger · Is this jolt worth a look? A candidate starts when the up-and-down shake jumps above the road's usual shaking. It only runs while all of these hold: moving at 9 km/h or more, phone not being handled, holder steady, GPS fix fresh. The speed check uses the GPS speed the phone has when the jolt starts; the speed shown for a jolt is read at its peak once the next GPS fix is in, so it can be a little lower (the "Below the bar" jolt: 7.9 km/h at its peak).
      • 02 · Measure · 50 numbers describe the jolt. About 2.5 seconds around the jolt become 50 measurements. These are the 12 the model relies on most, for this jolt.
      • 03 · Road defect? · 120 decision trees vote. Each square is one tree's vote; the outlined one is the tree opened in the figure. The chance it is a road defect is pothole + breaker + type unsure, averaged over all 120 trees. A bike needs 0.90 to report it, a car 0.70; autos and buses 0.80.
      • 04 · Which kind? · 100 more trees: pothole or speed breaker. A second forest only separates potholes from speed breakers. The type is shown only if it is at least 60% sure; between 40% and 60% it is too close to call, so the map says "type unsure" instead of guessing. A speed breaker must be crossed under 25 km/h.
      • 05 · The rider · What the rider sees. A report: "Pothole · Reported · 97% sure · beep", sent to the map as evidence, with its position, speed and probabilities. Or "No report": a felt jolt below the bar is still saved with the ride, kept in the ride's log only, so missed bumps can be checked later.

        How we testedmethod · caveats

        Offline tests on public recordings from other countries, and what the live map shows. Nothing here has been checked against surveyed Indian potholes yet.

        What was tested. Each public dataset (Indonesia, Bangladesh) was left out of training in turn, and the model was tested on it at the same false-alert rate: 1.5 per km. Pothole versus speed breaker was tested on a Korean car dataset it never trained on. The Brazilian PVS recordings are kept out of the false-alert counts.

        Bumps found on roads it never saw. Potholes and speed breakers found when each public dataset (Indonesia, Bangladesh) is left out of training, then tested at the same false-alert rate. The shipped model finds fewer than the plain jolt rule; the fixed recipe pulls a little ahead, offline only. Jolt rule alone: the trigger, no model. Shipped model: 2026.10.03, in the app. After four training fixes: offline only, not in the app yet.

        What it means. The shipped model finds fewer bumps on unseen roads than the plain jolt rule. A fixed training recipe pulls it slightly ahead, offline only. Telling the two types apart is the weakest step, so the map says "type unsure" rather than guess.

        Next validation step. Survey 60–100 local spots where the detectors disagree most, then grow them into a labelled set for the first Indian accuracy numbers. A retrained model ships only through the release gate.

        What the model knows. The model learned from public road recordings made by other teams in other countries. That is a real head start, and a real limit: it has never been taught by an Indian pothole. Road jolts, from three countries: 3,676 jolts from three public road datasets, recorded on motorcycles and cars; 2,893 carry the labels of the teams who recorded them, the rest count as normal road; 422 potholes, from Indonesia and Bangladesh; sideways and forward shake matter most: 3 of its top 4 measurements.

        What it doesn’t—yet: an Indian pothole. 0 training potholes from India: labelled rides on local roads come first, to measure the model here, then to train it. No accuracy figure for Indian roads until local spots are surveyed. Pothole or speed breaker from motion alone was a coin flip on a dataset it never trained on. Your rides teach it nothing yet: only reviewed bumps will count.
